Two figures that need their filing

Not visible in XBRL alone

The two most alarming numbers above both soften considerably once you read the 10-Q text behind them. Neither correction is available in the XBRL company-facts feed — both required parsing the filing itself.

Leverage is 4.84×, not 29.86×

29.86× gross → 4.84× recourse

Redwood bundles mortgages into securitisations that sit on the balance sheet under consolidation rules but are not the parent company's obligation — if those loans sour, the bondholders absorb it, not Redwood.

Counting only debt Redwood can actually be pursued for — $3.62B secured recourse plus $0.90B corporate, against $934M of equity — gives an ordinary number for a mortgage REIT. This is now tracked as its own series; see Exhibit 9.

The honest caveat on the caveat: recourse leverage has still nearly doubled since 2024, from 2.77×. The gross figure overstates the level dramatically, but it is not inventing the direction — and part of the rise is equity shrinking rather than borrowing growing.

The credit build is one run-off asset

$1.07M → $4.36M → $11.0M in two quarters

Effectively the entire $9.9M half-year build is a re-mark of a single Level 3 security: the subordinate interest Redwood retained when it sold $484M of legacy bridge loans into a wind-down vehicle during 2025.

The rollforward is explicit that no new securities entered an allowance position. Management cited worse-than-modelled workout outcomes on those legacy loans. Capital in that segment fell 19% in the quarter and is targeted below $100M by year-end — the exposure is ring-fenced and shrinking by design.

What to watch

Checkable each quarter

The consolidated loss is small and it is concentrated in one place. Segment results for CY2026Q2 break down like this:

Sequoia +32.1M Aspire +7.5M CoreVest +1.3M Redwood Investments +1.5M Corporate -20.4M Legacy Investments -23.1M ← winding down ────── Consolidated -1.1M

Strip Legacy and the operating businesses earn roughly +$22M a quarter. This is not a failing business — it is a working one carrying a drag it is actively removing. Legacy capital fell 19% in the quarter and is now under 10% of total capital, against a stated target of below $100M by the end of 2026.

That makes the outlook testable rather than a matter of opinion. Each signal below can be checked against this same data as new filings land.

Watch for What it would mean
Legacy Investments misses the sub-$100M target at end-2026 Adverse
The wind-down is slipping. Legacy is the whole gap between a loss and a profit, so a delay pushes breakeven out and makes further restructuring more likely.
The $0.18 dividend is cut Adverse
The first genuine distress signal. Everything else on this page is consistent with a difficult but managed transition; a dividend cut would not be.
Sequoia or Aspire production volumes fall materially Adverse
These two platforms are the growth engine and have posted consecutive record quarters. A sustained fall — most likely rate-driven — removes what currently offsets the drag.
Severance appears outside Legacy and CoreVest Adverse
Restructuring so far has been targeted at non-core and shrinking areas. Charges landing in the growth platforms or corporate functions would mean broad cost-cutting instead.
Equity stabilises and quarterly losses cross into profit Confirming
The repositioning working as intended. Book value per share has fallen for five straight quarters; arresting that is the clearest evidence the strategy is paying.

One limit worth stating plainly: segment results are a proxy for exposure, not a measure of it. A profitable segment can still reorganise, and a shrinking one can protect a function it depends on.

Built from a single call to the SEC CompanyFacts API (data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts) via get_company_facts.py, with matching plotting scripts. Duration measures — earnings, dividends, interest and net income — carry no standalone Q4 frame, so those quarters are backed out as Annual − Q1 − Q2 − Q3 and tagged as derived wherever shown. Balance-sheet measures are tagged every quarter and need no such treatment.

Recourse leverage (Exhibit 9) comes from a second pipeline, get_recourse_leverage.py, because the figure is absent from CompanyFacts entirely: secured recourse debt is stated only in MD&A prose, and is combined with corporate debt read from each filing's own XBRL instance. The two components reconcile against an independently tagged total debt figure to within 0.1%. Narrative context throughout was parsed from the Q2 2026 10-Q primary document, rwt-20260630.htm.

The two expense series (Exhibits 12 and 13) each cross a break in how Redwood tags its filings. Interest expense spans a straight concept rename, accepted only after confirming the old and new tags agree to the dollar on every overlapping quarter. Operating expenses lost its total tag altogether and is summed from four components for recent quarters, including two of Redwood's own custom tags that the API does not serve; the splice is likewise verified against the periods where both were reported. Four quarters of 2013 were tagged with inverted signs in a 2015 filing and are corrected here, flagged in the underlying data; one unverifiable negative was dropped rather than guessed at.
